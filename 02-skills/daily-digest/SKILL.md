# Daily digest

Read today's inbox and calendar, then write a five-line brief. Nothing is sent anywhere; the brief goes to the chat (or to `digest-YYYY-MM-DD.md` in the working folder if asked for a file).

## Steps

1. Get today's date from the system, not from memory.
2. Calendar: list every event today with start time, title, attendees and whether it has a video link. Note any two events that overlap.
3. Mail: pull every message received since 5:00 PM yesterday. For each one record who sent it, the subject, and whether it asks me for something (a reply, a decision, a document, a signature). A message that only informs is not an ask.
4. Match mail to meetings: if a sender is also an attendee of a meeting today, say so in the brief.
5. Write the brief in exactly five lines, in this order:
   - Line 1: the number of meetings today, the first start time and the last end time. Name any overlap.
   - Line 2: the one meeting that needs preparation and why (the most people, an external party, or a decision on the agenda).
   - Line 3: the asks in the inbox that are due today or have no date, sender and subject, oldest first. If there are more than three, name the three oldest and give the count.
   - Line 4: anything from a merchant, a bank partner or a regulator, named, even if it is not an ask.
   - Line 5: what has slipped: any ask older than three business days that has not been answered.

## Rules

- Plain sentences, no bullets inside the five lines, no headings, no greeting.
- Name people and companies as they appear in the message. Do not shorten merchant names.
- Dollar figures and dates exactly as written in the source; never round or restate them.
- If the calendar or mail cannot be read, say which one failed on line 1 and write the other four lines from what could be read.
- Do not reply to, forward, archive or label anything. Reading only.
